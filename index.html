Content Opportunity Scoring for Search Performance Review

A decision-support framework for identifying content observations that should be prioritized for refresh, investigation, or human review.

Fatima Iqbal · AI Software Engineer · Data Science · Machine Learning

View Results → View GitHub Repository
01 · Abstract

Research Summary

This study asks which observable search-performance signals can be combined into a repeatable approach for prioritizing content pages for review or refresh.

The analysis uses anonymized FlyRank search and analytics data and evaluates search visibility, traffic, engagement, demand, content age, and trend-related signals.

A Random Forest classifier was evaluated using both a conventional random split and a more conservative client-grouped validation design.

On the client-grouped test set, the model achieved 57.24% accuracy, compared with 54.21% for the majority-class baseline.

The results suggest that measured signals can provide directional decision support for content review, but they do not establish causal refresh effects or predict Google's ranking algorithm.

Research framing: This is a decision-support ranking system. It is not a causal model, a guaranteed refresh-success predictor, or a model of Google's ranking algorithm.
02 · Problem Statement

What Decision Does This Work Support?

Content teams can manage thousands of pages while search visibility, traffic, engagement, demand, and content age change at different rates.

The Content Review Problem

The central decision is not simply whether a page is performing well or poorly, but which pages deserve attention first.

A repeatable scoring framework can reduce the search space for human reviewers by combining multiple observable signals into a practical review queue.

Research Question

Which observable search-performance signals can be combined into a repeatable score to identify content observations that should be prioritized for refresh or human review?

Decision supported: The framework ranks observations that may deserve earlier investigation. It does not automatically determine which content action should be taken.

Opportunity Definition

A content opportunity is an observation whose measured signals suggest that it may deserve earlier human investigation.

Because there is no verified editorial ground-truth label, the score is a prioritization mechanism rather than a guaranteed prediction of refresh success.

Target / Label Definition

The predictive target is a binary distinction between declining and non-declining observations based on the available trend-direction field.

The target is treated as an observed performance outcome for prioritization, not proof that a page needs a refresh.

Validation Design

An original random split produced 70.13% accuracy. To test whether this estimate could be optimistic because related observations from the same client appeared across both training and testing data, a client-grouped split using GroupShuffleSplit was performed.

Records from the same client were kept together rather than appearing in both training and testing groups.

Leakage Checks

Target-derived and closely related outcome fields were excluded from predictive features.

trend_direction trend_pct impressions_last_30d clicks_last_30d sessions_last_30d impressions_prev_30d clicks_prev_30d sessions_prev_30d client_id content_id
Leakage principle: Variables that directly encode the target or its underlying outcome window were not used as predictive inputs.

What the Validation Result Tells Us

The Random Forest measured 57.24% accuracy under client-grouped validation, compared with 54.21% for the majority-class baseline.

This represents an improvement of approximately 3.03 percentage points.

The much higher 70.13% random-split result demonstrates why validation design matters when observations from the same client may be related.

Honest interpretation: The grouped result is the more conservative estimate of performance on unseen clients. The evidence supports directional prioritization, not a claim of universal generalization.

02

Accuracy fell from 70.13% under a random split to 57.24% when clients were kept separate.

03

Trend, engagement and search-demand signals provide useful context but are not universal rules.

06 · Ranked Recommendations

Content Action Playbook

The analysis supports a review-prioritization workflow rather than an automatic content decision system.

01

Review Declining Pages

Pages showing declining trend signals should receive early review, especially when recent sessions or other performance indicators also appear weak.

Reason: The trend signal showed directional evidence of changing recent performance, but the signal was classified as MIXED.

Recommended action: Inspect before deciding whether to refresh, improve, merge or monitor.

02

Review Pages With Weak Engagement

Pages with weaker engagement signals can be reviewed for content relevance, structure, readability and alignment with user intent.

Reason: Engagement-rate groups showed directional differences in recent sessions, but the relationship was not strong enough to classify as CONFIRMED.

Recommended action: Review content quality and user experience.

03

Review High-Demand / Weak-Visibility Pages

Pages associated with stronger search demand but weaker observed visibility may represent opportunities for further investigation.

Reason: Search-volume groups showed directional differences in impressions, although the heavy skew in search volume makes the relationship uncertain.

Recommended action: Review search intent, content coverage, internal linking, metadata and current positioning.

04

Review Aging Content

Older content can be monitored for potential refresh opportunities, especially when age is combined with declining or weak performance signals.

Reason: Content age is an observable signal, but the analysis cannot establish that age itself causes decline.

Recommended action: Compare older pages against recent performance before selecting a refresh action.

05

Monitor Instead of Automatically Changing

Pages with mixed or conflicting signals should remain in a monitoring queue rather than receiving an automatic action.

Reason: The signal audit found that the tested relationships were directional rather than universally reliable.

Recommended action: Collect additional observations and use human judgment before making a major content change.

Recommended operating principle: Use the ranking to decide where to look first, not what action must be taken. The final refresh, rewrite, merge, prune or monitoring decision remains with a human reviewer.
07 · Limitations

What This Study Cannot Claim

  • The dataset does not contain a verified ground-truth label showing whether a page actually required a refresh.
  • The analysis cannot prove that refreshing content causes search-performance improvement.
  • Search and traffic features can contain strong skew and extreme observations.
  • Model performance decreased from 70.13% on the random split to 57.24% under client-grouped validation.
  • The analysis measures associations in observed data rather than causal relationships.
  • The results should not be interpreted as a prediction of Google's ranking algorithm.
  • Human review remains necessary before refresh, rewrite, merge, prune or removal decisions.
Honest framing: The findings are best understood as observed and directional evidence that can support content-review prioritization.

10 · Final Summary

Content Opportunity Scoring

This study demonstrates a repeatable approach for using observed search-performance signals to prioritize content for human review.

The client-grouped evaluation shows a modest improvement over a majority-class baseline, while also demonstrating that random splits can provide a substantially more optimistic estimate.

The resulting framework should therefore be used as a practical review queue and decision-support system rather than an automated content decision engine.
