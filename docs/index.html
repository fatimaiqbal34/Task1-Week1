Content Opportunity Scoring for Search Review Prioritization

A data-driven analysis of observable search-performance signals for prioritizing content pages for review or refresh.

Author: Fatima Iqbal • Focus: Machine Learning & Search Intelligence
01 · Research Summary

Abstract

This study asks which observable search-performance signals can be combined into a repeatable score for prioritizing content pages for refresh or review.

Using anonymized FlyRank internship search and analytics data, the analysis evaluates search, traffic, engagement, content-age, and trend signals.

A Random Forest classifier is compared with a majority-class baseline using client-grouped validation and leakage checks.

The Random Forest achieved 57.24% accuracy compared with 54.21% for the majority baseline, while the original random split achieved 70.13%.

The results are treated as directional decision-support evidence rather than a causal explanation of Google's ranking system or a guarantee that refreshing content will improve performance.

02 · Research Context

Introduction & Problem Statement

Content teams may manage large collections of pages while having limited time to inspect every page with equal depth. A repeatable prioritization method can help direct human attention toward pages that show signals worth investigating.

This project investigates whether observable search-performance signals can help prioritize content pages for human review.

Research Question
Which observable search-performance signals can be combined into a repeatable score to identify content pages that should be prioritized for refresh or review?

Decision Supported

The analysis is designed to help prioritize which content pages should be reviewed first, based on observed search-performance patterns.

The resulting score is a decision-support ranking, not a claim about Google's ranking algorithm or a causal effect of refreshing content.

03 · Dataset

Data

This study uses the FlyRank internship warehouse release, with the analysis centered on the fact_content_daily_performance table.

The table is organized at daily content-page level. The analysis uses public-safe hashed identifiers and aggregate search-performance measures rather than client names, domains, URLs, private queries, or raw exports.

Primary Signals

  • gsc_impressions — observed search visibility
  • gsc_clicks — observed search clicks
  • gsc_avg_position — observed average search position
  • ga4_pageviews — observed page traffic
  • scroll_events — observed engagement activity

Excluded Signals

No client-identifying information, domains, URLs, private queries, credentials, or raw warehouse exports are included in this public research artifact.

Public-safe framing: Missing or unavailable measurements are not treated as evidence of poor content performance.
04 · Research Design

Methodology

The project uses a repeatable content-opportunity scoring approach. The goal is to identify pages that deserve earlier human review rather than predict Google's ranking system.

Feature Set

Opportunity Definition

A content opportunity is treated as a page whose observed signals suggest that it may deserve earlier review or refresh consideration.

There is no direct ground-truth field stating that a page definitely required a refresh. Therefore, the opportunity score is a prioritization tool rather than a verified prediction of refresh success.

Signal Audit

The distributions of the main numeric fields were inspected. Several features showed strong right-skew and heavy tails. Because extreme observations can strongly affect averages, the analysis uses medians, grouped comparisons, and directional interpretation where appropriate.

Baseline

30K Total observations
54.21% Majority baseline
57.24% Grouped model accuracy

The dataset contained 16,262 declining observations and 13,738 non-declining observations.

Proposed Model

A Random Forest classifier was evaluated as a stronger modelling approach for identifying declining content. The model used content, search, traffic, engagement, age, and categorical features while excluding identifiers and target-derived fields.

Validation Design

The original Week-5 random split produced 70.13% accuracy. To test whether that result was optimistic, a client-grouped split was performed using GroupShuffleSplit.

Records belonging to the same client were kept together rather than allowing the same client to appear in both training and testing groups.

Leakage Checks

Because the target label was created from trend_direction, target-derived or closely related fields were excluded.

  • trend_direction
  • trend_pct
  • impressions_last_30d
  • clicks_last_30d
  • sessions_last_30d
  • impressions_prev_30d
  • clicks_prev_30d
  • sessions_prev_30d
  • client_id
  • content_id

Model vs Baseline

The Random Forest measured 57.24% accuracy on the client-grouped test set, compared with 54.21% for the majority-class baseline.

This represents an improvement of approximately 3.03 percentage points over the majority baseline.

Random Split vs Grouped Split

70.13% Original random split

Key Findings

1. The model performed modestly above the majority-class baseline under client-grouped validation.
2. The random-split result was substantially higher than the client-grouped result.
3. The grouped result provides a more conservative estimate of performance on unseen clients.
4. The tested signals provide directional information that can support content-review prioritization.
5. The evidence is not strong enough to claim equal generalization across every new client or content portfolio.
06 · Responsible Interpretation

Limitations & Honest Framing

This analysis has several important limitations.

  • The dataset does not contain a verified ground-truth label for whether a page actually needed a refresh or whether a refresh successfully improved performance.
  • The declining-content label is therefore a proxy for review prioritization.
  • Several search and traffic features are highly skewed. Extreme observations can influence averages and model relationships.
  • Model accuracy decreased from 70.13% under a random split to 57.24% under a client-grouped split.
  • The analysis measures associations in observed data. It cannot establish causal effects.
  • Human review is required before making content, refresh, merge, rewrite, or removal decisions.
Honest framing: Results are described as observed, measured, directional, and intended for decision support. This research does not claim to predict Google's algorithm or establish causal refresh impact.
07 · Action Playbook

Ranked Recommendations

The analysis supports a review-prioritization workflow rather than an automatic content decision system.

Priority 01

Review Declining Pages

Pages showing declining trend signals should receive early review, especially when recent sessions or other performance indicators also appear weak.

Recommended action: Inspect the page before deciding whether to refresh, improve, merge, or monitor it.

Priority 02

Review Weak-Engagement Pages

Pages with weaker engagement signals can be reviewed for content relevance, structure, readability, and alignment with user intent.

Recommended action: Review content quality and user experience before making changes.

Priority 03

Review High-Demand Pages With Weak Visibility

Pages associated with stronger search demand but weaker observed visibility may represent opportunities for further investigation.

Recommended action: Review search intent, content coverage, internal linking, metadata, and current positioning.

Priority 04

Review Aging Content

Older content can be monitored for potential refresh opportunities, especially when age is combined with declining or weak performance signals.

Recommended action: Compare older pages against recent performance before selecting a refresh action.

Priority 05

Monitor Mixed-Signal Pages

Pages with conflicting signals should remain in a monitoring queue rather than receiving an automatic action.

Recommended action: Collect additional observations and use human judgment before making a major content change.
